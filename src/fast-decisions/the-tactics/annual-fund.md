**Problem:** Relying only on short-term donations can make it hard for an organization to plan for the future.

**Solution:** Build a strong annual fund with reliable, recurring donations from individuals and board members to ensure steady support year after year.

Focusing solely on immediate needs risks neglecting the long-term financial health of your organization. Developing and sustaining a robust annual fund is essential for building a stable financial foundation and should be viewed as a 30-year strategic investment, not a three-year goal. A well-established annual fund supports your organization's operational stability, providing the resources necessary to fulfill its mission year after year.

# **Tools:**

- Key Aspects of a Strong Annual Fund Strategy

- Commitment to Recurring, Reliable Support: Annual funds differ from short-term campaigns by generating renewable, reliable, and predictable income. They should be composed primarily of individual contributions and board gifts, supplemented by diverse funding but not dependent on it.
- Board Engagement as the Backbone of the Annual Fund:

- **Leadership by Example**: Board members should give generously, setting a strong precedent for other donors. Board contributions should account for a significant portion of the fund and serve as a model for annual giving.
- **100% Participation**: Achieving full board participation provides leverage in securing external funding, enabling the organization to approach donors from a position of credibility and strength.
- **Challenge Grants**: Increased board contributions can fuel challenge grants, encouraging other divisions or prospective donors to rise to the occasion.
- **Personalized Asks and Multi-Year Gifts**: A tailored approach with multi-year pledges can help maintain and elevate annual fund contributions.

- Structuring Revenue Goals by Division:

- Set specific targets for gross revenue, donor count, and average gift size by division to ensure accountability and measurable growth.
- Aim to secure 60-70% of annual fund revenue from individuals, with particular focus on board members and high-level donors, establishing people-powered funding.

- Key Practices for Growing and Sustaining an Annual Fund

- Front-Loaded Solicitation: To build momentum and avoid lag, front-load solicitation efforts, aiming to achieve 65% of the annual fund target within the first six months of the fiscal year. This phase should concentrate on:

- **Core Supporters**: Focus on renewing and lapsed donors, current and past board members, and frequent non-donating participants who show engagement with the organization.
- **Donor Growth and Retention Analysis**: Assess donor attrition across categories to understand where gaps exist, and identify the value of lost donors. Use historical data to predict new contributions and where additional efforts can have the most impact.

- Momentum Building: While the term “annual fund” implies a full year to reach targets, momentum is vital from the outset. Not all days or months are equally productive, so establishing a fast start will build sustained success.

- Distinctions in Annual Fund Allocations

- Avoiding Overreliance on Special and One-Time Contributions: While contributions from corporations, foundations, and government grants are valuable, they should not constitute the core of the annual fund, which should primarily support essential operating costs.

- **Operational Focus**: Annual fund income is directed toward meeting ongoing expenses like staffing, utilities, and core programs central to the mission.

- Embracing Major Gifts for Annual Fund Goals

- In the past, large gifts were associated primarily with capital campaigns. Today, securing major gifts is essential to meet annual fund targets and ensure operating budgets are fully funded. Integrating major gifts within the annual fund strategy can provide additional financial stability and demonstrate an enduring commitment to mission sustainability.
